Who should hold the cards?

Each person gets a card. No one can open your envelope alone: it takes several of them together.

How to choose
  • People you'd trust with your will. They will see everything in the envelope.
  • Spread them across households. If everyone lives together, they could open it early, and one house fire could lose every card.
  • Someone likely to outlive you, and someone organised who will actually act.
  • Your executor or solicitor already has a duty to handle your affairs.
  • Tell them. A card nobody knows about is a card nobody uses.

How your envelopes are protected

Nothing leaves your device

Tallystick does all its work inside your browser, on your own phone or computer. There's no account, no server and no tracking. What you type, the files you add and your finished envelopes are never uploaded anywhere, and nobody else can open your envelopes, including the people who make Tallystick.

  • It works offline. Once the page has loaded, it needs no internet at all. The downloadable file is built so it can't connect to anything.
  • Your draft stays in this browser. It's kept on this device so you can come back to it, and deleted when you seal.
  • You decide where envelopes go. They're ordinary files that you save, print or share yourself.
  • You can check. Switch on airplane mode before you start, or save the offline file and open it with the internet off. Everything works the same.

It's locked with a key only your cards can rebuild

When you seal an envelope, Tallystick makes a long random key and uses it to scramble everything inside. It uses AES-256, the same kind of encryption banks and governments rely on. Without the key, the file is meaningless noise. Guessing the key would take every computer on Earth far longer than the age of the universe.

The key is split between your card holders

The key is never written down whole. It's cut into pieces, one per card, using a well-tested method called Shamir's secret sharing. Think of a map torn so that any three pieces show the way, but two pieces show nothing at all. Fewer cards than you chose don't reveal part of the secret. They reveal nothing.

Mistakes and damage are caught

Each card code has a built-in check, so a typo is spotted straight away. If an envelope file is damaged or changed, it refuses to open rather than show the wrong thing. Before you see your cards, every combination of them is tested against the finished files.

What it can't protect against

  • Enough card holders agreeing to open it early. Choose people you trust, in different households.
  • Losing more cards than your spares. If too many are lost, nobody can ever open it.
  • Someone using this device while you're still filling it in. Your answers are kept here until you seal, then deleted.
